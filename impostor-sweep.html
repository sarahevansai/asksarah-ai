Why I built this

AI doesn't check whether a page is official.

It uses whatever page answers the prompt best. In Scrunch's study of AI answers from May to October 2026, impostor sites were cited for 83 Fortune 500 brands, and about three out of four of those answers presented the fake as the brand's official source. Refund and login prompts drew them most.

Most brands find a gap, not a fake.

A refund policy AI can't locate. An old support number still floating around. A contact page with no phone number written out. Those gaps are exactly how impostors get in, so the sweep finds them too.

This is a comms job.

Comms owns the official version of a brand's facts. Refund pages, support numbers and login help are reputation content now, because AI answers are where customers read them.

No. It only reads pages and looks up domain records. It never calls a number, logs in, fills in a form or downloads anything, and it tells you not to either.

Most brands won't have one. Almost every brand has gaps, and those are usually the most useful part of the report. When it does find something, every item is marked "suspected" until your security or legal team confirms it.
