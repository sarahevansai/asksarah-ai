How it goes

You don't read this. You do it.

Plan on about 15 minutes for a round. There's nothing to prepare first.

  1. Claude sets your seat

    It looks into you the way they would, fills in the news, the room and its best guess at the question you're dreading. You fix anything that's off and pick a heat.

  2. They ask, one at a time

    You answer the way you would in real life, out loud if you can. Sidestep and they ask again. Run long on live TV and the anchor cuts in.

  3. You get the scorecard

    The headline you wrote, the quote they'd pull, the clip that travels, a score out of 100 and the one fix that matters most.

  4. Run it again, hotter

    Same seat, new heat, no setup. Your heat ladder shows where your answers change as the pressure goes up.

Why I built this

The job changed. Most media training didn't.

The hard questions don't only come from traditional media anymore. They come from creators who build their own version of a story when there's no information out there. They come from employees, customers, analysts and legislators. Almost everything is recorded and transcribed, so almost everything is on the record. And the people interviewing you are prepping with AI, which can hand them something wrong about your company before you say a word.

Answer the question.

We used to train people to bridge. I'd push back on that now. Sidestepping is the thing that goes viral, and it can overtake every good part of the interview. The Hot Seat scores a straight answer higher than anything else.

Sound like a human.

The thing producers tell me they hate most is feeling like they're talking to a scripted bot. So the scorecard shows you where you sounded scripted, with a plain-spoken version of each line.

Ten rooms

Whoever is asking, there's a room for it.

  • Live TV hitAn anchor, four minutes, and clips cut from every answer.
  • Print interviewA reporter who has done the reading and is recording.
  • PodcastA friendly host, and 40 minutes to forget your point.
  • Crisis press conferenceSeveral reporters at once, streaming live.
  • PanelA moderator and rivals who also want the microphone.
  • Viral responseA creator has posted their own version of your story.
  • Town hallYour employees, after a hard announcement.
  • Customer callA customer after something went wrong.
  • Analyst briefingPeople who know your category and compare everyone.
  • Legislative hearingMembers with limited time, some asking for the camera.
